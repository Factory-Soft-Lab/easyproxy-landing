1. Who is responsible

The data controller is {{ co.name }}, a company based in {{ co.country }}, developer of {{ app }}. Anything related to this policy is handled at {{ co.email }}.

2. What we do not collect

To say it before anything else, {{ app }} does not:

  • ask for sign-up, email, phone number or password: there are no user accounts;
  • send captured traffic, your rules or your exports to any server of ours: we have none;
  • include analytics, advertising or attribution SDKs, or use advertising identifiers (no IDFA, no iOS tracking prompt);
  • read your location, contacts, photos, camera or microphone;
  • sell, rent or hand over data to anyone.

3. The traffic you capture

While capture is on, the app stores what it sees in the private database the system reserves for {{ app }}:

What is storedExample

The responsestatus code, headers and body
The connectiondomain, destination IP, port, timings, server certificate

That traffic may contain sensitive data from the apps you inspect: session tokens, passwords sent in clear text, personal data carried in a body. It stays on your device and we cannot see it. For an extra layer, turn on the app lock in Settings.

None of it is synced or copied to the cloud. On Android the app is also excluded from system backups.

4. The local VPN

{{ app }} uses the system VPN —NetworkExtension on iOS and VpnService on Android— because it is the only way for one app to see the traffic of others. It is a local VPN: traffic enters the app, is recorded on your device and goes out directly to its original destination. It is not routed to any server of ours or of third parties.

The VPN is not used to show ads, redirect traffic, collect data for other purposes or monetize it. It only turns on when you start capturing, and while it is on the system shows it (the VPN icon, and on Android a notification that cannot be dismissed).

5. The certificate

To read HTTPS traffic, the app generates its own certificate authority on your device. Its private key is kept in the iOS Keychain or the Android Keystore, never leaves the device and is not shared with any other. Only traffic for the domains you add in SSL Proxying is decrypted.

To install it, on iOS the app opens a local web server, reachable only from the device itself, from which the system downloads the profile. On Android it saves the public part of the certificate —never the key— to Downloads so you can install it from system settings.

7. What leaves because you decide

Some features send data off the device, always because you use them and to where you choose:

  • Export and share: a HAR, a request copied as cURL, a body, a mock flow. The file goes wherever you send it —email, messaging, your cloud— and from then on is governed by that service's terms.
  • Composing and replaying requests, WebSocket: the request you write or repeat is sent to the server it names, as any HTTP client would.
  • The rules you write (rewrite, map, block) change your apps' traffic to their own servers.

We receive no copy of any of it.

8. The speed test

The speed tool measures your connection against Cloudflare's public servers (speed.cloudflare.com), and only when you run it. To measure, it downloads and uploads filler data; like any web server, Cloudflare sees your IP address and the time of the connection, and receives nothing you have captured. Its processing is governed by Cloudflare's privacy policy.

9. Crash reports

When the app closes because of an error, a report is kept on the device itself: which version failed and where the code was. It carries no captured traffic. On iOS it arrives through MetricKit, only if you allow sharing analytics with app developers in system settings.

You can read those reports in full under More → Diagnostics, and they only leave the device if you share them.

10. Purchases

If you buy {{ pro }}, the transaction is processed entirely by Apple or Google. We never see your name, email or payment details: the store tells the app whether Pro is active, and nothing else. That data is handled by the store you paid in.

11. Who it is shared with

No one, on our initiative. The only third parties involved are those already named: Apple or Google for purchases and the operating system, and Cloudflare if you run the speed test. We use no ad networks, analytics SDKs or additional third-party services.

12. How long it is kept

  • Traffic, rules and crash reports: on your device, until you delete them or uninstall the app.
  • On our systems: nothing, because nothing reaches them. If you email support, we keep the email as long as needed to help you.

13. Children

{{ app }} is a tool for professionals, is not directed at children under 14, and does not knowingly collect data from them.

14. Your rights

Under Colombian Law 1581 of 2012 and its regulations, you have the right to know, update and correct your data, to be informed about its use, to file complaints with the Superintendence of Industry and Commerce, to revoke authorization and to request deletion. If you live elsewhere —for example, in the European Union— you also have the rights your law gives you.

In practice you exercise them yourself: we hold no data that identifies you, and what the app stores is on your device, where you can view, export or delete it at any time. For anything else, write to {{ co.email }} and we will reply within the legal deadlines.

15. How to delete everything

  • Captured traffic: Settings → Clear captured data.
  • Everything in the app: uninstall it. There is no account to close because there never was one.
  • The certificate: uninstalling the app does not remove it. On iPhone, Settings → General → VPN & Device Management → {{ app }} → Remove Profile. On Android, Settings → Security & privacy → More security & privacy → Encryption & credentials → Trusted credentials → User. Menu names may vary by manufacturer.

16. Changes to this policy

If something relevant changes we will update this page and the date in the header. If the change affects what leaves the device, we will also announce it in the app before it takes effect.
