1. Who we are

{{ app }} is developed and distributed by {{ co.name }}, a company based in {{ co.country }} ("we"). You can write to us at {{ co.email }}.

2. What {{ app }} is

{{ app }} is a development and debugging tool: it shows the network traffic of the apps on your own device —requests, responses, headers, bodies, timings— and lets you change it to test what you build: rewrite responses, pause requests, simulate a slow network or serve fake responses.

In particular, the app is not:

  • a privacy or anonymity VPN: it does not hide your IP or encrypt your connection from third parties;
  • a service that routes your traffic through our servers: we have none;
  • a tool for monitoring other people or their devices.

3. Which traffic you may use it on

Use it only on traffic you have the right to inspect: your own apps, apps you test with their owner's permission, or your own device to understand what it does. You alone are responsible for having that right.

Intercepting or decrypting other people's communications without authorization is a crime almost everywhere. In Colombia it is covered by Law 1273 of 2009 (interception of computer data and abusive access to a computer system). {{ app }} is not made for that and we do not support that use.

Some apps and services forbid inspecting or altering their traffic in their own terms. Complying with them is up to you.

4. The VPN and the certificate

To see traffic, {{ app }} turns on a local VPN, which is the way iOS and Android let one app see the traffic of others. Traffic continues directly to where it was going; the VPN does not divert it to any server.

To read encrypted (HTTPS) traffic for the domains you choose, the app generates its own certificate authority on your device, which you install and trust by hand. While it is installed and trusted, {{ app }} can decrypt the traffic of those domains. Understand what that means:

  • the certificate's private key stays on your device and never reaches us;
  • someone holding your unlocked device could use the app to read that traffic;
  • when you stop using the app, remove the certificate: the app explains how in Settings.

Many apps reject user-installed certificates (certificate pinning) and stop connecting when their traffic is decrypted. That is those apps' decision, not a defect in {{ app }}.
